Hello Raffle Nexus team,

I run Lottizen (https://lottizen.com), a free, independent site about Canadian lotteries. Next to draw-game results and scratch-ticket data, it now covers charity lotteries: hospital home lotteries, team 50/50s and Catch the Ace. It tracks 91 of them so far, organized by province. Several of the largest run on your platform, including the Heart & Stroke Lottery, SickKids Lottery, the BC Children's Hospital Dream Lottery, the VGH Millionaire Lottery and the London Dream Lottery.

Today we take their figures only from each charity's own rules page. Your Terms of Use say that automated access to the Platform needs RNC's prior written approval. So before we read anything from Raffle Nexus pages, such as current 50/50 payouts, prize lists, draw status and published winning ticket numbers, I'm asking for that approval, on terms that suit you.

What we'd show for each lottery:
- Name, charity, licence number, licensing province, ticket packages and deadlines
- The current 50/50 jackpot
- Winning ticket numbers once they're published

All of it is information that's already public on the lottery's own pages. We never store or show winners' names.

What we'd send your lotteries:
- **Visitors from the right province.** Each lottery has a page on Lottizen's province page (for example https://lottizen.com/charity/british-columbia). The "Buy tickets" link goes straight to the lottery's own order page on your platform, and only visitors whose province matches the licence see it. There's no affiliate or tracking parameter, and we take no payment per ticket. This follows the provincial rules on seller payment and on marketing outside the licensing province.
- **Email subscribers.** Readers can follow a lottery with just an email address. We email them 3 days before each Early Bird or Final deadline, saying which draw a ticket bought later will miss. We also email when the lottery is close to selling out, and when winning numbers are published. Each email links back to the lottery.
- **Search traffic.** Each lottery with published results gets a winning-numbers page, which is the search people run after a draw.

How we'd access the data:
- At whatever cadence and through whatever interface you prefer: the public pages, a JSON feed you provide, or only the lotteries whose charities opt in.
- We'd identify our requests with a named user agent and keep the volume low.
- Each listing would credit Raffle Nexus as the platform, if you'd like that.
- You or any charity can ask us to remove a listing at any time.

If you're open to this, a short written reply granting approval, with any conditions you want attached, is all we need. I'm also happy to talk it through on a call.

Thank you for considering it.
