New York runs some of the country's most-played games — [New York Lotto](/usa/new-york-lotto), [Take 5](/usa/take-5), [Numbers](/usa/numbers), [Win 4](/usa/win-4), and [Pick 10](/usa/pick-10) — and it's also the single most expensive state in America to win a big prize. Here's how to claim, and what the state and city will take on the way.

## Where to claim, by prize size

| Prize amount | Where you claim |
| --- | --- |
| $600 or less | Any licensed retailer (paid immediately) or a Customer Service Center |
| $601 and up | A Customer Service Center **by appointment**, or by mail to Schenectady |
| Large prizes | Mega Millions over $500,000, or Powerball of $50,000+, are forwarded to the Schenectady office |

Small prizes are paid on the spot at retailers. Above $600, you claim at one of roughly **16 Customer Service Centers** statewide — and **appointments are required**; walk-ins aren't accepted, so book online or by phone first. A useful exception: the **Long Island Customer Service Center** has a secure **Prize Claim Drop Box** you can use for any amount, no appointment, during business hours.

Prefer mail? Send the **signed original ticket**, a copy of your ID, and a completed claim form to **New York Lottery, PO Box 7533, Schenectady, NY 12301-7533.** The largest prizes are handled at that Schenectady headquarters.

## The one-year deadline

For **draw games**, you have **one year from the date of the winning drawing** to claim. **Scratch-off** games run on their own clock — you have up to a year after the game's announced end/redemption date, which differs game to game. As always, **sign the back of the ticket immediately**; it's a bearer instrument, and a signature is what ties it to you.

## What to bring

- **Your signed winning ticket.**
- **A completed Winner Claim Form.**
- **Valid government-issued photo ID.**
- **Your Social Security or taxpayer ID number** — required for tax reporting; New York issues a W-2G on prizes over $600 and withholds on prizes over $5,000.

Bring photocopies of the ticket and form for your own records, because the originals aren't returned.

## Why New York is the costliest state to win in

This is what sets New York apart. Stack the layers on a prize over $5,000:

- **Federal:** 24% withheld up front (and a jackpot ultimately owes up to 37% — the withholding is only a prepayment).
- **New York State:** **10.9%**, the **highest state lottery rate in the country**. (If you see an old 8.82% figure, it's outdated — that was the pre-2021 top rate.)
- **New York City residents:** an additional local tax of about **3.876%**.
- **Yonkers residents:** a further resident surcharge on top of the state tax.

Put together, a **New York City** winner faces roughly **10.9% state + 3.876% city** on top of federal withholding, and at the top 37% federal bracket the **total effective tax can exceed 50%** of the prize. No other state comes close. The mechanics of federal and state lottery tax generally are in [Lottery Taxes in the US](/guides/lottery-taxes-us-federal-and-state). (For the exact current New York City and Yonkers withholding percentages, confirm against the state's Publication 140-W before relying on a specific number — the local surcharges are updated periodically.)

## Can you stay anonymous?

By name, no. New York law makes the winner's **name, city or town, and prize amount public record**, and winners may be expected to take part in publicity. There's no statutory anonymity.

The available workaround is the **trust or LLC**: New York permits claiming through a legally formed entity, so the **entity's name** is what gets published rather than your own. A real example — a large New York prize once appeared publicly as *"Precious Blessings LLC."* Two caveats: the **entity name is public** (so keep personal identifiers out of it), and you must still provide your **personal SSN** for tax reporting. Set the entity up **before** you claim, with a New York attorney. The broader state-by-state picture is in [Which States Let Lottery Winners Stay Anonymous?](/guides/anonymous-lottery-winners-by-state). (A New York bill to add opt-in disclosure protections has been introduced but is not law as of 2026 — New York remains a disclosure state.)

## One more thing: watch for scams

Because New York publishes winners, big winners become targets. The New York Lottery is clear that it "never requires you to pay money to claim your prize," and that "processing fees" are a myth. Anyone who contacts you demanding a fee to release winnings is running a scam — see [Lottery Scams: How to Spot Them](/guides/lottery-scams-how-to-spot-them).

## The short version

Under $600, any retailer; above it, a Customer Service Center by appointment or a mailed claim to Schenectady; the biggest prizes at headquarters. Sign the ticket, bring photo ID and your SSN, and claim within a year. Then brace for the tax — New York, and especially New York City, takes more than anywhere else in the country — and decide on a trust *before* you claim if privacy matters.
