Loto-Québec's claim process has two features that set it apart from the rest of Canada: an unusually wide **online claim window** that runs all the way to $50,000, and **direct-deposit payouts** — Interac and bank deposit — where most other corporations still mail a cheque. Here's how to claim, tier by tier.

## Where to claim, by prize size

| Prize amount | Where you claim |
| --- | --- |
| $600 or less | Any retailer |
| $600.01 – $1,999.99 | Online, at a claim centre, or by mail |
| $2,000 – $24,999.99 | Online, claim centre, or mail (signed claim form required) |
| $25,000 – $50,000 | Online, or by appointment at Montreal HQ |
| Over $50,000 | By appointment only, Montreal HQ |

The notable feature is that **online claims run from $600.01 all the way to $50,000** — a far higher online ceiling than OLG ($10,000 fully online) or BCLC ($24,999.99). At **$2,000 and above** you'll need to complete and sign a claim form; above **$50,000**, plus annuity prizes and televised-draw participation, it's an appointment at head office.

Loto-Québec's **claim centres** operate by appointment in **Montréal (HQ), Québec City, Gatineau, Trois-Rivières, La Malbaie, and Mont-Tremblant**. Mailed claims go to **Loto-Québec, PO Box 7777, Succ. Centre-ville, Montréal, QC H3C 4L4**. One correction to a common myth: larger prizes are **deposited to** your bank account — you don't cash them *at* a bank branch (that's Atlantic Canada's system, not Quebec's).

## Check your ticket before you claim

Loto-Québec's advice to winners starts with two steps: **sign your ticket on the front, and photograph both sides** (or photocopy it). Signing helps show you're the owner and protects you if the ticket is lost; the photo gives you a record. Then verify the result through an official channel — the ticket verifier in the Espacejeux app, an in-store terminal, or the posted numbers — before deciding which claim route applies. Don't rely on a glance at the numbers.

## The one-year deadline

Draw lotteries give you **one year (12 months) from the draw date** to claim. Instant/scratch tickets are handled differently — their claim window is tied to the end of the game rather than a fixed year.

## What to bring

For a claim of $2,000 or more:

- **Your original ticket, signed and completed front and back.**
- **A completed, signed claim form.**
- **A copy of valid photo ID** — a driver's licence, RAMQ health card, or passport.
- **Your banking information**, only if you're choosing direct deposit or Interac.

Loto-Québec does **not** ask for your SIN.

A detail worth getting right: the ticket must be **signed and completed on both the front and back**. Loto-Québec's rules treat the legitimate holder of a signed ticket as the owner, so completing it fully is what ties the prize to you before you ever reach a claim centre. If you're claiming by mail rather than online, use registered or trackable mail for anything valuable — a ticket lost in transit is treated like any other lost ticket.

## What happens after you file

A small prize claimed online or at a retailer is quick. A larger claim — especially above $25,000, or an annuity prize — is handled by appointment at head office and involves verification before the money moves. Because Loto-Québec pays by **deposit** rather than a mailed cheque, once a claim clears, the payout itself is fast: an Interac e-Transfer Autodeposit or direct bank deposit lands in your account without waiting on the post. The trade-off for that speed is that your banking details must be in order and the account must be in your name.

## How you'll be paid

This is where Quebec differs most. Loto-Québec offers four payout methods: **Interac e-Transfer Autodeposit, direct bank deposit, deposit to a lotoquebec.com account, or a cheque** (the slowest option). Deposits must go to a **Canadian account in the winner's name**. Most other corporations pay large prizes by cheque only, so if fast, paperless payout matters, Quebec's system is the most convenient in the country.

The prize is a single, **tax-free** lump sum — the ILC regulations confirm lottery prizes aren't included in income. The exception, as elsewhere, is a "for life" game: **Gagnant à vie** pays either **$1,000 a week for life** or a non-taxable **$1,000,000 lump sum**, and its annuity is paid net of taxes. For the full tax treatment, see [Are Lottery Winnings Taxable in Canada?](/guides/are-lottery-winnings-taxable-in-canada).

## Claiming as a group

The threshold to watch is $2,000. If the **group's total prize is under $2,000**, it's paid to the group leader, who distributes it. At **$2,000 or more**, Loto-Québec pays members **individually**, and requires the original signed **group agreement (convention de groupe)** signed by all members — plus, for each member whose share is $2,000 or more, an individual claim form and a copy of their ID. Because members are paid individually, documenting the split in advance is clean and simple. If you play in a pool, [Group Play and Office Pools](/guides/lottery-group-play-office-pools-canada) covers how to keep it dispute-free.

## Publicity

Quebec is often wrongly assumed to allow anonymous wins. It doesn't — the ILC regulations make publicity consent a condition of payment, with no dollar threshold, so it applies at every prize level. Loto-Québec still publishes full names in its releases (first names only appear on social media and cheques). The full province-by-province picture is in [Can You Stay Anonymous After Winning?](/guides/lottery-winner-anonymity-canada).

## The short version

Under $600, any retailer; up to $50,000, claim online for a direct deposit; above $50,000, an appointment in Montreal. Bring your signed ticket and photo ID, add banking details for a fast Interac or bank-deposit payout, mind the one-year clock — and receive the full amount, tax-free.
