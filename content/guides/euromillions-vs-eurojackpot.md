Europe has two continent-spanning jackpot games, and they look almost like twins: same numbers to pick, same draw nights, the exact same odds of hitting the jackpot. Yet [EuroMillions](/europe/euromillions) and [EuroJackpot](/europe/eurojackpot) differ in ways that genuinely change which one is the better bet for you. We track 1,962 EuroMillions draws back to 2004 and 971 EuroJackpot draws back to 2012 — here's how the two actually compare.

## The one thing that's identical: jackpot odds

Both games use the same matrix — **pick 5 from 50, plus 2 from a pool of 12** (Lucky Stars in EuroMillions, Euro numbers in EuroJackpot). Because the math is identical, so are the jackpot odds: **1 in 139,838,160** for each, a figure stated verbatim on both games' official odds pages. If your only question is "what are my chances of the top prize," the two games are indistinguishable.

## Where they diverge: your odds of winning *anything*

This is the first real difference. EuroMillions has **13 prize tiers** — including a bottom "Match 2 main numbers" prize — while EuroJackpot has **12**. That extra tier lifts EuroMillions' overall odds of winning *any* prize to about **1 in 13**, versus roughly **1 in 32** for EuroJackpot. So while the jackpot is equally hard in both, EuroMillions pays out *something* far more often.

| Any-prize odds | ~1 in 13 | ~1 in 32 |

## Bigger ceiling vs wider reach

The two headline trade-offs:

- **EuroMillions goes higher.** Its jackpot is capped at **€250 million** — more than double EuroJackpot's **€120 million** cap — and it starts higher too (€17M vs €10M). If you're chasing the largest possible European prize, EuroMillions is the game.
- **EuroJackpot is cheaper and broader.** At a **€2 base ticket** (versus €2.50), it costs less per line, and it's played across **19 countries** spanning Central, Northern, and Eastern Europe, against EuroMillions' **9** mostly Western European members. More countries means a larger, more frequent stream of jackpots, even if each ceiling is lower.

A useful quirk: **Spain plays both games**, so Spanish players can choose between them draw for draw. Most other countries offer one or the other.

## So which should you play?

It depends on what you're optimizing for:

- **Chasing the biggest possible jackpot?** EuroMillions, with its €250 million cap.
- **Want the best chance of winning *something*?** EuroMillions again — 1 in 13 versus 1 in 32.
- **Want the cheapest ticket per line?** EuroJackpot, at €2.
- **Just playing whatever's available where you are?** That's usually the real deciding factor — most countries carry one game, not both.

Whichever you play, remember the rule that governs both: the jackpot may be pan-European, but you **claim in the country where you bought the ticket**, under that country's rules and taxes. A win taxed at 0% in the UK is taxed at source in Spain or Portugal — the mechanics are the same for both games, and they're covered in [EuroMillions: How It Works Across 9 Countries](/guides/how-euromillions-works-across-countries) and [Spain and Portugal Lottery Tax](/guides/spain-and-portugal-lottery-tax).
