Collecting a UK National Lottery or [EuroMillions](/europe/euromillions) prize is more structured than most people expect, and the process forks in two directions depending on whether you bought a paper ticket in a shop or played through an online account. The single number to remember is **£50,000** — cross it and the friendly automated process gives way to a phone call and an in-person visit. Here's the full path, and the deadline you can't miss.

## Who you're claiming from

Since **1 February 2024**, the National Lottery has been run by **Allwyn Entertainment**, which took over the licence from Camelot. For players, the games, the app, and the claim process carry on much as before — but you'll see Allwyn's name behind the operation now.

## Paper tickets: where to claim by prize size

If you hold a physical ticket or Scratchcard:

| Prize amount | Where you claim |
| --- | --- |
| Up to £100 | Any National Lottery retailer |
| £100.01 – £500 | Retailers with a Post Office counter (at their discretion) |
| £500.01 – £50,000 | Online claim form, then post the ticket to The National Lottery |
| Over £50,000 | By phone, then validated and paid **in person** by appointment |

Small prizes are paid over the counter. Note a change under Allwyn: since February 2024, **Post Office branches no longer pay prizes over £500.** For prizes from **£500 up to £50,000**, you complete an **online claim form** and then send your ticket in to The National Lottery to be validated.

Above **£50,000**, it becomes personal. You call the Customer Care Team (0333 234 50 50) to validate the claim, complete a **Prize Claim Form**, and Allwyn arranges to process the prize **in person — at your home or another agreed location in the UK or Isle of Man.** This is the tier that jackpots fall into, and it's handled discreetly and by appointment.

## Online players: it's mostly automatic

If you played through your National Lottery account, the prize is already tied to it, so there's no ticket to post:

| Prize amount | How you're paid |
| --- | --- |
| Up to £500 | Automatically credited to your National Lottery account |
| £500.01 – £30,000 | You confirm payment to your registered debit card (within the claim period) |
| £30,000.01 – £50,000 | Contact Customer Care to validate; paid by cheque |
| Over £50,000 | Same in-person process as paper tickets |

For online play, everything up to £500 simply appears in your account balance. Between £500 and £30,000 you confirm the payout to the debit card registered to your account — and there's a hard cutoff: you must confirm before **11pm on the last day of the claim period.**

## The 180-day deadline

This is the rule that costs careless players their prizes: you have **180 days to claim.** For draw games, that's 180 days from the **draw date**; for Instant Win games, 180 days from **purchase**; for Scratchcards, 180 days after the **game closes.** Miss the window and, as the National Lottery puts it, your entitlement to the prize is simply lost.

And that money doesn't disappear into the operator's pocket — an **unclaimed prize, plus the interest it earned** while held in trust, goes to the **National Lottery good-causes** projects funded across the UK. So an unchecked ticket is, in effect, an accidental donation. The fix is the usual one: check tickets promptly and don't let them pile up.

## What you need

- **Paper ticket:** the physical winning ticket or Scratchcard — it's your proof of the claim. For any prize over £50,000, you'll also complete a Prize Claim Form.
- **Online play:** nothing to post — the win sits in your account, and you claim through the account itself.

For a large prize, sign the back of a paper ticket immediately and keep it safe; like anywhere, it's the ticket that proves the win.

## After you claim: the good news

Once the prize is validated and paid, the UK's biggest advantage kicks in: **the money is entirely tax-free.** There's no withholding and nothing to declare on the prize itself — a UK jackpot is paid in full. What happens *after* — tax on the income it earns, and inheritance tax if you give it away — is covered in [UK Lottery Winnings and Tax](/guides/uk-lottery-winnings-and-tax). And unlike most of the world, you can also keep the whole thing private: see [Can You Stay Anonymous in the UK?](/guides/staying-anonymous-uk-lottery).

## The short version

Under £500, claim at a shop; up to £50,000, online then post the ticket (or straight to your account if you played online); over £50,000, a phone call followed by a private, in-person payout. Whatever the amount, the clock is **180 days** — claim inside it, or the prize goes to good causes. Then enjoy the rare luxury of a jackpot that arrives whole, tax-free, and — if you choose — completely anonymous.
