Ontario runs the most digital prize-claim system in the country — OLG was the first Canadian lottery to let you claim a five-figure prize entirely online — but the route your ticket takes still depends entirely on how much it's worth. Here's exactly where to go, what to bring, and how you'll be paid, whether you've hit a [Lotto 6/49](/canada/lotto-6-49) jackpot or a smaller [Ontario 49](/canada/ontario-49) or [Lottario](/canada/lottario) win.

## Where to claim, by prize size

OLG splits claims at the $1,000 line:

| Prize amount | Where you claim |
| --- | --- |
| Under $1,000 | Any OLG retailer (the store pays at least the first $50 in cash) |
| $1,000 – $10,000 | Fully online, by mail, or at the Toronto Prize Centre |
| $10,000 – $49,999 | Online or by mail, with added verification |
| $50,000 and up | In-person appointment at the Prize Centre |

Anything **under $1,000** is paid at the counter of any OLG retailer, subject to how much cash the store has on hand (the balance can come as debit/credit credit or Free Play). Once you cross **$1,000**, retailers can no longer pay you — the claim moves to OLG itself.

The standout feature is the **fully online claim** for prizes between $1,000 and $10,000: you complete OLG's online claim form end-to-end, upload photos of your signed ticket and ID, and OLG mails a cheque in roughly a week. Prizes of **$50,000 or more** require an in-person interview by appointment. OLG.ca online-game and iGaming wins skip all of this — they're credited straight to your player account.

Everything that isn't online funnels to one place: the **OLG Prize Centre, 31 Adelaide Street East, Toronto**. Unlike the western corporations, OLG has no regional prize offices — mail and in-person claims all go to Toronto.

## Check your ticket before you claim

Don't rely on a glance at the numbers. Sign the ticket first — a retailer can't even validate an unsigned ticket — then verify the win through an official channel: a retailer's ticket-checker, the OLG Lottery app, or OLG's Check Winning Numbers page. A retailer terminal will flag a winner, but confirm the *amount* yourself rather than trusting the store's read, especially on a larger prize. If the ticket is worth $1,000 or more, the terminal will tell the retailer the prize can't be paid in-store and direct you to claim through OLG — that's expected, not a problem with your ticket.

## The one-year deadline

You have **exactly one year (52 weeks) from the draw date** to claim a prize on a ticket printed at a lottery terminal. INSTANT (scratch) games are different — they expire on the date printed on the back of the ticket. Miss the window and the prize is forfeited to the unclaimed-prize fund, so check tickets promptly rather than letting them pile up.

The upside of a year-long window: there's no reason to rush a big claim. Take the time to read [What to Do If You Win the Lottery in Canada](/guides/what-to-do-if-you-win-the-lottery-in-canada) and line up advice before you file.

## What to bring

For any claim of $1,000 or more you'll need:

- **Your signed original ticket** — one signature, no photocopies, and no extra marks or white-out (stray writing can trigger an investigation and delay payment).
- **Valid government photo ID.**
- **A completed Lottery Prize Claim Declaration form**, which is built into the online claim flow.
- **A Release of Prize Claim form** if you're claiming a ticket you didn't buy.

One OLG-specific quirk: it **does not accept your SIN as identification** and asks you not to submit it with your claim — but it may require your SIN later, during processing of a large prize. Claims of $1,000 or more are also screened against Family Responsibility Office arrears (a support-payment check, not a tax).

## How you'll be paid

OLG pays by **mailed cheque**. Online claims of $1,000–$10,000 arrive in about a week; mail-in claims take six to eight weeks. OLG.ca wins go to your player account.

And the number on the cheque is the number you won: Canadian lottery prizes are **tax-free**, and OLG withholds nothing. There's no US-style annuity to weigh on a standard jackpot — it's a single lump sum. (A few "for life" games like [Daily Grand](/canada/daily-grand) let you *elect* an annuity instead, but that's the exception.) For the full tax picture, see [Are Lottery Winnings Taxable in Canada?](/guides/are-lottery-winnings-taxable-in-canada).

## Claiming as a group

If you pooled tickets, OLG uses a group version of the Declaration form plus a **Lottery Prize Claim Group List**, signed in ink by every member, on which each person declares their share. The Group Leader submits the claim and OLG follows up to collect each member's details. If your pool is an office or friends arrangement, read [Group Play and Office Pools](/guides/lottery-group-play-office-pools-canada) first — a documented split at claim time is what keeps a shared win from becoming a dispute.

## If someone gave you the ticket

Because a ticket behaves like a bearer instrument, a ticket you were *given* is yours to claim once you hold and sign it — but OLG wants the paper trail to be clean. If you're claiming a ticket you didn't buy, OLG asks for a **Release of Prize Claim form** from the person who did, so it's clear the prize is legitimately yours. The same applies to a ticket bought as a gift or won on someone else's behalf. Sorting this out *before* you file avoids the most common cause of a held-up claim: a mismatch between who's named on the claim and who bought the ticket.

## What can hold a claim up

Most OLG claims are straightforward, but a few avoidable things slow them down. An **unsigned ticket**, or one with **extra marks, notes, or white-out**, can trigger an investigation. **ID that doesn't match** the signature on the ticket is another. And every claim of $1,000 or more is checked against **Family Responsibility Office** support arrears before payment — a support-enforcement step, not a tax, but one that can pause a cheque. Clean ticket, matching ID, one signature: that's the whole recipe for a fast payout.

## Publicity: plan to be named

Ontario is not a place you can win quietly. OLG posts the **name and city** of every winner of $1,000 or more on OLG.ca for 30 days, and prizes of **$10,000 or more require a published winner photo**. As of May 2026, OLG drops last names from its *news releases*, but full names still appear on its website and the photo requirement is unchanged. If privacy matters, read [Can You Stay Anonymous After Winning?](/guides/lottery-winner-anonymity-canada) before you claim — the short answer in Ontario is no.

## The short version

Under $1,000, walk into any retailer. Between $1,000 and $10,000, claim online and watch for a cheque in about a week. Above that, expect verification, and above $50,000, an appointment in Toronto. Bring the signed ticket and photo ID, mind the one-year clock, and remember the prize arrives whole and tax-free — with your name attached.
