British Columbia's claim system is built around a tier of **Regional Prize Payout** offices, with the biggest wins reserved for two head-office locations. Where you go depends entirely on the size of the prize — whether it's a modest [BC/49](/canada/bc-49) win or a seven-figure jackpot. Here's the full path.

## Where to claim, by prize size

| Prize amount | Where you claim |
| --- | --- |
| Under $200 | In person at a lottery retailer (cash) |
| Up to $2,000 | Any lottery retailer (subject to store cash) |
| $200 – $24,999.99 | Online, or at a Regional Prize Payout (RPP) office |
| $25,000 and up | By appointment, BCLC office in Kamloops or Vancouver |

Small prizes are paid at retailers. From **$200 up to $24,999.99** you have two good options: claim **online** through the PlayNow portal (BCLC mails a cheque in about 7–21 days), or visit a **Regional Prize Payout office** — BCLC operates these around the province, in places like Burnaby, Coquitlam, Vancouver, Nanaimo, Victoria, and Kamloops. RPP offices handle prizes up to $24,999.99 (with $10,000–$24,999.99 claims taken Monday to Saturday, 8–4).

The hard line is **$25,000**. At that level and above, you **must** claim in person, by appointment, at one of just two BCLC offices — **Kamloops** (head office) or **Vancouver** — and the prize is paid by cheque. There's no online or mail option for the big ones. PlayNow.com account wins are handled in-account and don't touch this process.

## Check your ticket before you claim

Sign the ticket first, then verify the win through an official channel — a retailer's ticket-checker, the PlayNow app, or the posted winning numbers — rather than trusting a glance at the numbers. BCLC also gives a reason to photograph the signed ticket right away: if it's later lost, "you will have evidence to support your claim for an investigation." Thirty seconds at the counter turns a potential dead end into an investigable claim.

## The one-year deadline

BCLC gives you **one year (52 weeks) from the draw date** to claim, "unless otherwise announced." Scratch tickets follow the expiry date printed on them instead. A found-too-late ticket is a lost prize — BCLC is explicit that a winning ticket surfacing after 52 weeks can no longer be claimed.

## What to bring

- **Your signed winning ticket** — the name and signature should match your ID.
- **One valid government photo ID** showing your photo, date of birth, signature, and expiry (driver's licence, BCID, passport, or PR card).

What you *don't* need is also worth knowing: BCLC does **not** require your SIN or banking information to claim a ticket prize, because large prizes are paid by cheque rather than deposit. For a group win of $10,000 or more, you'll need a designated Trustee and a Group Prize Agreement (below).

Note that large claims are also security-reviewed as a matter of course: BCLC's Corporate Security team reviews all claims of $25,000 or more (and retailer claims over $2,000) and interviews the claimants — a standard integrity step, not a sign of a problem.

## What happens after you file a big claim

A large BC win isn't paid on the spot — it's verified first, and that's by design. BCLC's Corporate Security team reviews **all claims of $25,000 or more** (and retailer claims over $2,000), and interviews the claimants. BCLC can also review any claim "at any time if there are concerns about the ownership of a winning ticket." This is the integrity backbone of the system, not a sign anything is wrong, and it's why the big claims are in-person and by appointment. Bring a matching signature and ID, expect an interview, and the review is routine.

## If someone gave you the ticket

Because a lottery ticket is effectively a bearer instrument, a ticket you were given is yours to claim once you hold and sign it — but the signature on the ticket should match the ID you present. If there's any question about who owns a ticket (a gift, a shared purchase, a workplace pool), BCLC's ownership review is where it gets sorted, so it pays to have the story and paperwork straight before you file. For pooled tickets specifically, see the group process below.

## How you'll be paid

BCLC pays by **cheque** — mailed for online claims, issued at the office for in-person claims. There is **no direct deposit** for ticket prizes. The amount is the full face value: BC prizes are **tax-free** and nothing is withheld, and a standard jackpot is a single lump sum with no annuity option. For the complete tax picture, see [Are Lottery Winnings Taxable in Canada?](/guides/are-lottery-winnings-taxable-in-canada).

## Claiming as a group

For a group ticket, appoint a **Group Trustee** (19 or older) who is treated as the lawful holder of the ticket. Whenever a group ticket wins **more than $2,000**, BCLC requires a **Group Prize Agreement** in which every beneficiary declares their share and attests they aren't a retailer or BCLC employee. Only the original, marked group ticket is accepted. If you play in a pool, read [Group Play and Office Pools](/guides/lottery-group-play-office-pools-canada) — a real BC case (a $2-million jackpot fought over in 2025) turned entirely on whether a written pool agreement existed.

## Publicity: consent is mandatory

BCLC requires **written consent** to publish your personal information, and states plainly that "if a prize claimant refuses such consent, a prize may be withheld." Major-prize claims include a photo and an interview. As of January 2026, BCLC publishes only a first name, last initial, photo, and story — but that's a softening of the format, not anonymity. Genuine anonymity is granted only rarely, for a substantiated public-safety concern; a BC winner of $30 million was granted it in 2018, reportedly one of only a handful of approvals in years. The detail is in [Can You Stay Anonymous After Winning?](/guides/lottery-winner-anonymity-canada).

## The short version

Under $2,000, any retailer; up to $24,999.99, an RPP office or online for a mailed cheque; $25,000 and up, an appointment in Kamloops or Vancouver for a cheque in person. Bring your signed ticket and one photo ID, mind the one-year clock, expect a security review and publicity on a big win — and receive the full amount, tax-free.
